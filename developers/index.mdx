speaks.

**Everything downstream of acceptance is identical.** Same routing, same rating, same approvals, same
receipts. The choice affects how you hand a message over, not what happens to it.

## Three things that surprise people

Worth knowing before your first integration rather than during your first incident.

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="A success response does not mean delivered">
The submit response is sent **before routing runs**. So `200` — or `ESME_ROK` — means *accepted for
routing*, and nothing more.

A message can be accepted and then refused, dropped or fail to route, and the only way you learn is a
[delivery receipt](/developers/receive/delivery-receipts). If your integration treats acceptance as
delivery, it will be wrong in exactly the cases you care about.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Your sender ID and your wording may both need approving">
On an account with whitelisting enforced, an unapproved `from` is refused, and so is a message body
that matches no approved template.

This is normal for regulated destinations — Indian DLT most visibly. If your sends work in testing
and fail in production, this is the first thing to check.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Underscores and hyphens are the same field — except once">
`dlr_url` and `dlr-url` are one field, and sending **both** is a `400` rather than last-one-wins.

The single exception is `custom_tlvs`, which keeps its underscore. `custom-tlvs` is an unknown
argument.
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>
