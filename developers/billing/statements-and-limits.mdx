## Your usage history

Every message produces a **call record** — what you sent, when, what it cost, and how it finished.
Your statement is that history rolled up by period.

Both live in the customer portal rather than on the API. See
[the portal](/developers/portal/messages-and-statement) for reading and exporting them.

<Tip>
There is **no REST endpoint for your message history**. `/secure/balance` gives you the current
number; the portal gives you the history and a CSV export. If you need history in your own systems,
export it rather than trying to reconstruct it from receipts — receipts you failed to receive are
exactly the ones you would be missing.
</Tip>

## What a record holds

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| The message | Destination, sender ID, segments, encoding |
| The money | Unit rate, segment count, total |
| The outcome | Final state and when it was reached |
| The TLVs | **The ones you sent** — not what a vendor added downstream |

You see your own side of the message. What supplier carried it, and what they added to it, is not
yours to see — that is the same rule the `vendor` field on a receipt follows.

## Segments, not messages

**You are billed per segment.** A record shows the segment count, and the total is the unit rate
multiplied by it.

This is where an unexpected bill usually resolves: a body that tipped over 160 GSM-7 characters, or a
single curly apostrophe that forced the whole message to UCS-2 and dropped capacity to 70. See
[Encoding and segments](/developers/send/encoding-and-segments).

## The two ceilings

They are different things and produce different failures:

| Limit | Exceeded by | You get |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Messages per second** | Sending too fast | `429`, or `ESME_RTHROTTLED` over SMPP |
| **Credit** | Sending more than you have funded | `402 Insufficient credit` |

<Warning>
**Neither has a header telling you when to try again.** There is no `Retry-After` on a `429` — back
off exponentially with jitter, because a fixed interval across several workers re-synchronises them
into the next burst.

And a `402` should not be retried at all. Retrying cannot add credit; it just turns one alert into
thousands.
</Warning>

## Refusals cost nothing

Every refusal — unapproved sender, unmatched template, missing TLV, insufficient credit — leaves you
charged nothing, or refunded if the charge had already been taken.

That is worth knowing when reconciling: **the count of messages you sent and the count you were
billed for are legitimately different numbers**, and refusals are the gap.

The one case that surprises people is a multipart content refusal, where every segment was
acknowledged before the message was dropped — you see acknowledgements for segments that were then
refunded.

## What a `500` means for your bill

`500 Cannot send message` reverses any charge. That is why it is the one server error safe to retry:
the retry costs one message, not two.
