---
title: "Billing FAQ"
description: "Reconciliation, refunds, disputed messages and the numbers that legitimately disagree."
---

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="I sent 1000 messages and was billed for 1240. Why?">
Segments. You are billed per segment, not per message, and 240 of yours were longer than one.

A message over 160 GSM-7 characters splits at 153 per segment. If anything in the text forces UCS-2 —
a curly apostrophe is the usual culprit — the limits drop to 70 and 67, so a 150-character message
becomes three segments.

`/secure/rate` returns `submit_sm_count` for any body, free, before you send it.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="I sent 1000 and was billed for 960. Is that a mistake?">
No — refusals cost nothing. Unapproved sender IDs, unmatched templates, missing mandatory TLVs and
insufficient credit all leave you charged nothing, or refunded if the charge was already taken.

**The count you sent and the count you were billed for are legitimately different numbers.** Refusals
are the gap, and your provider can list them.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why am I being refused when I have money on the account?">
Almost always a **refill in flight**. Credit is reserved in blocks and refills are asynchronous, so a
message arriving mid-reservation is refused with money still there. Retrying succeeds.

**The block size does not strand credit** — a reservation falls back to the single message that asked
if that is all the balance covers, so you spend every unit you hold.

A refusal that persists on a funded account is a different problem; raise it.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do I get refunded for a failed message?">
It depends where it failed:

| | Charged? |
| :--- | :--- |
| Refused at ingress | **No** — nothing was taken |
| Accepted, then dropped before a vendor | **Refunded** |
| Sent to a vendor and refused by them | **Refunded** |
| Delivered | Charged |
| `UNDELIV` or `EXPIRED` after a real attempt | **Charged** — the attempt was made |

The last row is the one worth internalising: a handset that was switched off for a day costs you the
message, because your provider paid to try.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A customer says they never got a message I was billed for">
Look at the final state on the record. `DELIVRD` means the carrier reported delivery — beyond that is
a query for the carrier, and `operator_msg_id` is the handle you quote when raising it.

If the state is `UNKNOWN` or the message settled as `EXPIRED` with no receipt ever arriving, ask your
provider whether receipts are requested at all on that route. **If every message on a route settles
as `EXPIRED`, nothing is reporting outcomes** and the delivery rate is meaningless rather than zero.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why is a partly-delivered long message billed in full?">
Because every segment was submitted and paid for. Resolution reports the **worst** segment's outcome,
so the message reads FAILED — but the segments that did go were still carried.

Billing follows what was sent, and delivery reporting follows what the recipient could read. They are
answering different questions.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can a retry double-charge me?">
Only if you retry something that actually succeeded. There is **no idempotency key** on
`/secure/send`, so a retry after a timeout sends a second message and charges for it.

Retry only on `429` and `500`. A `500` is explicitly safe — the charge is reversed before you get it —
but a **timeout is not**, because the message may have been accepted and you simply never saw the
response. Keep your own de-duplication key for anything user-visible.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does a quote cost anything?">
No. `/secure/rate` sends nothing, charges nothing, and counts against no rate limit. It is the only
genuinely free pre-flight check on the API.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why does the same message cost different amounts to different countries?">
Pricing is per destination — country and often network. That is why `to` is required on a quote: a
price without a destination would be a guess.

`product` can change it again, if your provider has allowed your login more than one.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How far back does my history go?">
Your provider's retention policy decides that, and records are purged on a schedule. If you need
history beyond it, **export on a schedule of your own** rather than assuming it will be there.
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>
