---
title: "Billing FAQ"
description: "The questions operators ask once money is moving — discrepancies, stranded credit, unpriced traffic and periods that will not close."
---

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="An account is refusing messages and has money on it">
Almost always a **refill in flight**, not stranded credit.

Credit is reserved in blocks, and refills are asynchronous by design — so a message arriving while the
next reservation is being taken can be refused with money still on the account. Retrying succeeds.

**The block floor does not strand anything.** A reservation tries the full block, then the
message-denominated floor, then just the single message that asked, so an account spends every unit it
holds. One funded for three messages sends three.

If this happens on a **well-funded** account, the cause is different and historic: the floor used to
be a currency amount, which could size a block at exactly one message for an expensive destination.
That is what `smsg.balance.block.min.messages` exists to prevent.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A top-up did not arrive">
The account almost certainly has no `account_balance` row. A `RECHARGE` for such an account credits
nothing, stays **pending**, and applies itself the moment the row appears — with a WARN naming the
account.

**Creating a credential does not create a balance row.** Use *Open for credit* on the account page.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Reconciliation says an account is out by exactly its consumption">
You are using the wrong identity. `credited − consumed = balance + reserved` is **wrong** and fails
this way on every account.

Consumption never touches the balance columns — `reserved` is a lifetime-consumed counter, not a
hold. The correct pair is:

```
Σ(RECHARGE + TRANSFER) = balance + reserved
reserved − consumed    = reserved but not yet spent  (≥ 0)
```
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Credit is stuck in reserved after a crash">
A `kill -9` strands it. `balance + reserved` is still conserved — **the money is parked, not lost** —
and `POST /ops/credit/release` returns it. A clean shutdown does this automatically.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Traffic is being sent and nothing is charged for it">
An account nobody has priced is **not refused** — it sends unpriced and uncharged, with one line at
`debug`.

Set the `[*]` scope. It prices any account with no rule of its own, including accounts created later,
and it is the single highest-value thing to configure on day one. Without it, this failure is silent
and cumulative.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A vendor's margin shows as unknown rather than a number">
Working as intended. **A rule that matches nothing leaves the amount unknown, never zero** — and the
`[*]` default applies to *price only, never to cost*.

If it defaulted on the cost side too, every margin would compute to zero rather than unknown, and
nothing would tell you a vendor had no rates.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A postpaid account has stopped sending and is under its limit">
Its period probably has not been closed. The most recent `BILL` row is the start of the unbilled window — **closing is what returns headroom.**

Nothing is broken; the bill has not been cut.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A refund landed after I closed the period. Do I reopen it?">
No — a closed period is never rewritten. The panel re-runs the summary, compares it against the
stored figure, and shows the difference as a **credit note against the next period**.

Restating silently would mean the invoice the customer paid last month stops matching what the panel
shows this month, with nothing recording that it moved or why.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The purge refused to run">
It found a day with no `usage_daily` row. That is a refusal of the whole run, by design — deleting an
unsummarised day destroys the consumption record permanently.

Run `fireflo usage rollup --catch-up`, then purge. The two are separate commands deliberately: a
purge that quietly rolled up first would mean a rollup bug is discovered by the command that deletes
the evidence.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Daily totals are off by a few hours of traffic">
`FIREFLO_BILLING_ZONE`. A billing day is a **local** day, and on UTC the last five and a half hours of
an Indian customer's evening land on the next day's line.

Set it before the first rollup. Changing it afterwards means deleting and recomputing every day
already summarised.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A message I know was sent appears in no list">
Usually the writer and the reader are pointed at different databases. Check `smsg.cdr.sink` and the
gateway's `FIREFLO_DB_URL` against the panel's `METRICS_DATABASE_URL` before looking anywhere else.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Revenue looks too high after a burst of vendor refusals">
It should not, and if it does, check the query. A refused message carries **no money** — `price`,
`cost` and `margin` are null, because credit was returned at the moment of refusal.

Every built-in revenue query sums those columns *without* an outcome filter, precisely so a refunded
message cannot overstate takings. A hand-written query treating `NULL` as zero breaks that.
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>
