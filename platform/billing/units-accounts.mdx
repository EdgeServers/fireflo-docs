---
title: "Units accounts"
description: "An account that holds message units instead of money: who pays for its messages, how units are counted, what a customer sees when they run out, and how fast it may send."
---

From **0.16.0** a child account can be a **units account**. It holds message units per product and no
money. Its messages are charged, in money, to its parent.

It exists for a reseller that sells a customer "5,000 SMS" rather than an amount of credit: the
customer can send exactly what it was given, over SMPP and the HTTP API alike, and the gateway counts
it as the messages are sent.

## Who pays, and who is limited

| | The units account | Its parent |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Holds** | Units, per product. Never money | Money: a prepaid balance or a postpaid limit |
| **Prices** | None of its own | The parent's tariff prices every message |
| **Each message part** | Takes **one unit** of the login's product | Is charged at the parent's price |
| **Billing records** | Named beside the message, as its units account | Is the account the message is billed to |

A send needs both: a unit on the units account **and** credit on the parent. Units are a limit, not
prepaid money. A three-part message takes three units.

A units account is always a direct child of an account that holds money. A units account cannot have
children of its own.

## How units are counted

Units are counted by the same engine that counts [prepaid credit](/platform/billing/prepaid-credit):
each gateway node reserves a block, spends it from memory with no database call per message, and
settles what was spent. The count is exact after a crash.

- **A refunded message gives its unit back**, once: a vendor refusal, an expiry, a drop, a message
  that could not be queued.
- **Small allowances are shared fairly.** A node never reserves more than its share of what is free,
  and hands back a block it has not used for a few seconds, so five units on a two-node gateway are
  five sendable messages, whichever node the customer reaches.
- Units are enforced wherever prepaid credit is. On a gateway with prepaid enforcement off, a units
  account's logins are not loaded and cannot bind or send: nothing would be counting them.

## What a customer sees when it runs out

| Situation | Reason recorded | SMPP | HTTP API | gRPC |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| No units of its product left | `noUnits` | `0x403` (`conf.status.noUnits`) | `402`, code `NO_UNITS` | `FAILED_PRECONDITION`, `NO_UNITS` |
| Its units are held by another node for a moment | `unitsPending` | `0x14` | `503`, code `UNITS_PENDING`, `Retry-After: 5` | `UNAVAILABLE`, `UNITS_PENDING` |
| It has units, its parent has no credit | `insufficientCredit` | `0x402` | `402` | `FAILED_PRECONDITION`, `INSUFFICIENT_CREDIT` |
| It has units, its parent's credit is not confirmed in time (0.17.x; a retry goes through) | `creditPending` | `0x58` | `503`, code `CREDIT_PENDING`, `Retry-After: 2` | `UNAVAILABLE`, `CREDIT_PENDING` |

Nothing is charged for any of the three, and in the third the unit is given back.

`0x403` is deliberately not the no-credit `0x402`: a customer can tell "I am out of units" from "my
provider is out of money". The status is set per listener with `conf.status.noUnits`, in hex or
decimal; the default is `0x00000403`.

`unitsPending` is the only one worth retrying: units held elsewhere come back within seconds.

## Where units accounts come from

A units account is created, given units and taken units from by **the platform that manages it**
(FireFlo OMNI, for example) over the gateway's management API. The control panel shows units accounts
and their ledger; it does not create them or move units.

Every give, take-back and move is written to an append-only unit ledger under a reference, and a
repeat with the same reference changes nothing. For every account and product, free + reserved + used
equals the sum of its ledger.

A platform can create units accounts only while prepaid enforcement is on **and every running gateway
node is 0.16.0 or newer**. During a rolling upgrade that begins once the last older node is gone.

An existing account is never converted. A money account stays a money account; a units account is a
new account under the parent.

Money cannot be moved to or from a units account: top-ups, debits and credit transfers naming one are
refused.

## What a units login cannot do

A units login is not its parent on the self-service API.

- **Balance** answers its own units, never the parent's money — see
  [Balance](/reference/api/balance#a-units-login).
- An accepted send does not show the parent's price.
- The rate quote, credit transfers, sender ID and template registrations and the batch list answer
  `403` with code `UNITS_LOGIN`. Those belong to the account that pays.

## How fast it may send

A units account can have a speed limit of its own, on top of its parent's. The platform that manages
it sets it; two values, either of which may be left unset.

| | SMPP | HTTP (REST, gRPC) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Key** | `conf.maxRate.<units account>` | `smsg.restapi.maxRate.<units account>` |
| **Scope** | Per listener; in a cluster each node enforces its share | Per node, its share of the cluster |
| **What it limits** | Submits a second from every session of every login of the units account. Each part of a long message is one | Send requests a second by the units account's logins. One per message |
| **Over the limit** | `0x58` (`ESME_RTHROTTLED`) | `429`, code `RATE_LIMITED` |

**Precedence.** A submit is checked against the units account's own limit first. Over it, the submit is
refused without drawing on the parent's limit, so one customer sending too fast does not use up its
reseller's allowance. Within it, the parent's limits apply as they always did: the parent's own
`conf.maxRate`, the listener's default and the port's budget. A login with a faster limit of its own is
still held to its units account's limit.

`0x58` means "slow down"; `0x14` means "try again shortly". Both are resent by a well-behaved client.
`0x58` is not only the speed limit: from 0.17.x a money account's message whose credit was not
confirmed in time (`creditPending`) answers `0x58` too, by default — see
[Status codes](/reference/smpp/status-codes). A units login's own refusals are unchanged: `noUnits` is
`0x403` and `unitsPending` is `0x14`.

Empty or `0` means the units account has no limit of its own, and only the parent's apply.

A units login's binds count against its parent's connection limit. There is no separate bind limit per
units account.

## In the control panel

A units account's page says **units** in place of a balance and names the account it is charged to.
Its **Billing** tab shows units left and used per product, its logins, units used this month and by
day, its speed limits and the unit ledger. The Tariff and Statement tabs are not shown: prices and the
statement are the parent's. No money action is offered.

A parent's **Billing** tab lists its sub-accounts, with units left per product and a "no units" state.
If units accounts cannot be used yet, because a node is older than 0.16.0 or prepaid enforcement is
off, the tab says which.

Refused messages appear on [Refused](/platform/control-panel/refused) under **No units left** and
**Units being reserved; try again**. The account shown there is the parent, which pays; the login is
the units account's.

## Upgrading to 0.16.0

The schema moves from 1.129.0 to 1.131.0, so update with `--migrate`. It adds empty unit tables and one
nullable column on the call record. An installation with no units accounts behaves as it did on
0.15.13. One key is new, `conf.status.noUnits`; nothing has to be set.

Update the control panel with the gateway: an older panel reports the schema as newer than it expects.
