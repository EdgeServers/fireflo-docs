title: "Controlling a worker"
description: "Three pairs of verbs that apply immediately, what each does to traffic in flight, and the one that will eventually refuse new messages."
---

Stored configuration alone is **too slow to be a control**. The gateway notices a database change on
its next poll, up to thirty seconds later — and toggling something off and back on inside that window
nets to no difference at all, so the gateway never hears about it.

These verbs apply immediately.

```bash
./scripts/fireflo disable vendor1
./scripts/fireflo suspend vendor1
```

## They mean different things on a vendor and on a listener

Both kinds of worker take the same verbs, and **the same verb does a different job on each** — which
is the point. A vendor is somewhere traffic goes; a listener is where traffic comes from, and it also
carries receipts back to a customer. So "stop sending" on one is not the same act as on the other.

<Warning>
**Before 0.9.21 the controls did not act on a listener's own traffic at all.** Stop sending and Hold
on a listener paused only the loop carrying receipts back to bound customers; submitted messages kept
going out. If you are running an earlier build, this is the release to take.
</Warning>

### On a vendor

| Verb | What it does |
| :--- | :--- |
| `suspend` / `resume` — **Stop sending** | No more messages go to this vendor, including any already queued and any part-way through a retry. New traffic **fails over to your other vendors**, so total outbound volume does not drop. The session stays bound. |
| `restart` — **Restart** | Disables and re-enables it in one act, so the sessions are torn down and re-dialled. For a vendor that is bound but not behaving. |
| `enable` / `disable` | Stops it outright and **disconnects** it. A disabled worker leaves `/ops/health` entirely. |

### On a listener

| Verb | What it does |
| :--- | :--- |
| `suspend` / `resume` — **Stop sending** | Submits are still **accepted** (`ESME_ROK`) and charged, then **parked** rather than routed. **Receipts keep being delivered** throughout. Resume routes everything parked, in arrival order and once each. |
| `hold` / `release` — **Hold** | Every submit is **refused at the door** with `ESME_RMSGQFUL`, **before any charge is taken**, so a refused message costs nothing. Receipts are held too. Binds stay up, and well-behaved clients retry — so releasing resumes traffic without anyone reconnecting. |
| `enable` / `disable` | Stops both directions and drops the binds. |

## Choosing between them

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="Stop sending on a vendor — it is misbehaving and you have another">
The right default for a vendor problem. New traffic finds an alternative route immediately and the
session stays bound, so you can test it without a fresh bind.

**Expect total outbound volume to stay flat**, because the traffic went somewhere else rather than
stopping — look at the other vendors' counters, not the total, to confirm it worked.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Stop sending on a listener — you want the messages, just not yet">
For a short window where you would rather hold a customer's traffic than refuse it: a route being
edited, a vendor being swapped. The customer sees nothing wrong, and every message is delivered when
you resume.

<Warning>
**Parked messages are charged and are held in memory.** The customer has been told the message was
accepted, so it is owed — but the parked copy does not survive a restart, and the parked count is
capped. Past the cap the listener refuses further submits before charging them. Watch the parked
count on the worker's health block and do not leave a listener parked for long.
</Warning>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Hold on a listener — you want the traffic to stop arriving">
The stronger control, and the safer one for anything longer than a moment. Nothing is accepted,
nothing is charged, and nothing accumulates on this gateway. The customer's own stack holds their
backlog and retries, which is where a backlog belongs.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Disable — take it out of service entirely">
The worker leaves `/ops/health` completely, which is worth knowing before you go looking for it in a
dashboard and conclude something has crashed.

Use it when you want the worker gone rather than paused.
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>

<Warning>
**A held vendor's queue grows, so watch `queue_depth` and `queue_capacity`.** Under a capacity limit
the gateway eventually **refuses new traffic** rather than growing — reaching customers as
`ESME_RMSGQFUL` or a `503`. Holding is not free; it converts a vendor problem into an ingress problem
if you leave it long enough.
</Warning>

## These are runtime state, not configuration

<Warning>
**A disabled or suspended worker returns on restart** unless the stored configuration says otherwise.

These verbs exist to act *now*; they do not edit the configuration. If you want the change to survive
a restart, make it in the panel as well — and if you want it *only* until the restart, that is exactly
what these give you.
</Warning>

## Testing a vendor before you trust it

```bash
fireflo test-bind vendor1
```

Dials once and reports the outcome, without waiting for the reconnect cycle.

<Tip>
**It is refused while the vendor is already bound**, deliberately. A second bind on the same
`systemId` can cost you the live session, and many carriers allow only one.

Use it before traffic depends on the vendor, or after suspending it — suspending keeps the session,
so suspend then test is *not* the sequence that works. Disable, then test.
</Tip>

## Dropping one customer's bind

A listener session can be disconnected by id. A **vendor** session cannot — that answers `409`,
because the guard is the worker kind rather than the presence of a session id.

Disabling a login also drops its live binds, which is the difference between "they cannot reconnect"
and "they are gone now".
