<Warning>
**This page is about the gateway itself, not the messages it carries.** Nothing here counts messages,
deliveries or vendor throughput — those are on the [dashboard](/platform/control-panel/dashboard) and
[Live health](/platform/operations/live-health). This is whether the software has the memory,
processing power and connection capacity to keep doing its job.
</Warning>

That separation is the whole design. A gateway can be delivering perfectly while quietly running out
of file descriptors, and it can be idle while leaking memory. Neither shows up in a delivery figure.

## Recent trend

Four sparklines — memory, processor, tasks running, work waiting — labelled **since this page
opened**. The history is held in the browser tab and starts empty, so it genuinely cannot show you
anything from before you arrived. For a longer view, point a monitoring system at the gateway's own
measurements endpoint.

## The five verdicts

Each card carries a badge — **Healthy**, **Worth watching**, **Under pressure**, or **Not
reported** — a summary, a fill bar, and two lines of prose: what the reading means, and **the rule
the badge was reached by**.

Publishing the rule is deliberate. A threshold you cannot see is a threshold you cannot argue with,
and these are judgements about your platform, not laws of nature. If 70% processor is normal for your
traffic shape, the card is wrong and you should know precisely how wrong.

| Card | Healthy | Worth watching | Under pressure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Memory** | below 75% of the ceiling | to 90% | above 90% |
| **Processor** | below 70% | to 90% | above 90% |
| **Connection capacity** | below 70% of the limit | to 85% | above 85% |
| **API requests** | under 250 ms average | to 1 second | above 1 second |
| **Internal task queue** | below 70% of slots busy, nothing waiting | anything queued | — |

### What each one is actually telling you

**Memory.** Memory that keeps climbing and never falls back after a cleanup is how a leak looks. Run
out entirely and the gateway stops accepting messages. The detail line counts cleanup pauses and the
total time spent paused.

**Processor.** Sustained high use means throughput is limited by this machine rather than by the
vendor. Brief spikes during a burst are normal.

**Connection capacity.** Every SMPP session, database connection and open file counts against this.

<Note>
Reach the connection limit and the gateway **refuses new vendor and customer connections before any
other symptom shows**. Nothing about the traffic looks wrong first. This is the card that fails
quietly, and the reason it is on the page at all.
</Note>

**API requests.** Messages submitted over HTTP, plus this panel's own calls. Slow responses here
point at the gateway or its database, not at a vendor. The figure is an average since the process
started — useful for "this is slow in general", useless for a spike a moment ago.

**Internal task queue.** The gateway's own background work — database writes, receipt handling. Work
waiting here that never clears means it is arriving faster than it can be done, and the detail line
will tell you if any was turned away outright, which is work the gateway could not do at all.

<Warning>
**The internal task queue is not a worker pool.** *Worker* means an SMPP connection everywhere else
in this documentation and everywhere else in the panel. It is avoided here on purpose: readers who
met the word on this card went looking for a vendor that was never involved.
</Warning>

Where a reading is simply absent, the card says so in words — "The gateway did not report a memory
ceiling" — rather than showing a zero that reads like a measurement.

## The raw readings

One control, **Show the raw readings**, opens everything the gateway publishes, in the software's own
names, grouped by subject. This is the same data a monitoring system would scrape, and it is here for
investigating something the cards above cannot explain.

There is a single search box across every group rather than one per card, because when you are
searching you generally do not know which group the measurement is in — that is most of why you are
searching. Groups matching nothing disappear rather than leaving empty headings behind.

The step down is one step on purpose. An earlier version dropped a reader from plain English to raw
label syntax in a single click, which is not progressive disclosure so much as a cliff.

A footer reports when the page last read, and refreshes every ten seconds.
