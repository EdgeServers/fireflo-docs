The control panel is the operator's half of FireFlo. Your customers never see it — they get
[the portal](/developers/portal/signing-in), which shows them their own traffic and nothing of yours.

Nothing here is required to run the gateway. The gateway reads its configuration from a database or
from files and would keep carrying traffic if the panel were switched off entirely. What the panel
gives you is a way to change that configuration without hand-editing, and a way to see what happened
without writing SQL.

## The sidebar

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="Overview — the first screen">
Traffic, delivery and failures across the whole gateway, over 24 hours, 7 days or 30 days. Start
here in an incident; it is the only screen that answers "is anything wrong" without you having to
name the thing you suspect.

See [the dashboard](/platform/control-panel/dashboard).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Gateway — the things that carry traffic">
| Menu item | For |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Messages** | Every message, searchable, with a detail view per message |
| **Vendors** | Outbound connections — who you buy from |
| **Servers** | Inbound listeners — how customers connect to you |
| **Accounts** | Your customers, and everything hung off them |
| **Pricing** | Tariffs, both what you charge and what you are charged |
| **Diagnostics** | Logs and configuration read from the gateway's own machine |

**Vendor** and **server** are the two halves of the same idea, and mixing them up is the most common
early mistake — see [connections](/platform/connections/vendors).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Configuration — rules the gateway applies">
**Filters** are reusable conditions that routing rules and tariffs both draw on. **TLVs** is the
catalogue of SMPP optional parameters, including ones your customers have proposed and you have not
yet approved.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Assets — what customers may send">
**Headers** are sender IDs; **Templates** are approved wording. Both are the operator side of what
customers request in their portal, and both are only enforced where you have switched enforcement on.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Reports — what happened, and what it was worth">
| Menu item | Answers |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Refused** | What was turned away before it became traffic |
| **Queues** | What is waiting right now |
| **Receipts** | How well each vendor reports outcomes |
| **Usage** | Volume and delivery, by account |
| **Revenue** | Billed, cost and margin |
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>

## The screens with no menu entry

Six screens exist that the sidebar never lists. They are reached from other screens, which is fine
once you know and baffling until you do.

| Screen | Reached from | What it is |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Live health** | The heart icon in the header | Binds, queue depth and in-flight receipts, right now |
| **Runtime** | "Runtime detail" on the Overview health strip | Memory, processor and connection capacity |
| **Routing** | The Vendors screen | Routing tables, rules and vendor groups |
| **Products** | The Servers screen | The product catalogue, and per-product rates |
| **Message detail** | Any row on Messages | One message end to end, including its TLVs |
| **Sign-in** | Signing out, or an expired session | The operator sign-in form |

<Note>
**Runtime and Live health answer different questions and are easy to confuse.** Live health is *what
is true right now* — which vendors are bound, how deep the queues are. Runtime is *whether the
software has room to keep working* — memory, processor, file descriptors. A gateway can be perfectly
healthy on one and in trouble on the other.
</Note>

## Inside an account

Opening an account gives you seven tabs, and they are genuinely different subjects rather than
sections of one page:

| Tab | Holds |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Overview** | Traffic and delivery for this customer alone |
| **Settings** | Who they are, whether they are listed, and where they sit in a reseller tree |
| **Logins** | Their SMPP and HTTP credentials |
| **Approvals** | Sender IDs and templates they have requested |
| **Billing** | Credit, the ledger, and reconciliation |
| **Tariff** | What this customer is charged |
| **Statement** | What they owe for a period |

The header above the tabs shows either **Balance** or **Limit**, never both — prepaid accounts hold a
balance, postpaid accounts have a credit limit, and reading one as the other is the misreading that
costs money.

<Warning>
**The Billing tab is the only place in FireFlo where credit is created, and a top-up cannot be
undone.** Read [the Billing tab](/platform/control-panel/accounts-billing) before you use it.
</Warning>

## Signing in

Operator sign-in is a username and password held in the panel's own configuration — there is no user
table, and no way to add a second operator from inside the panel. See
[the control panel's configuration](/reference/config/control-panel).

Sessions are per browser. Signing in on a laptop does not sign you in on a phone, and signing out
ends only the session you are using.
