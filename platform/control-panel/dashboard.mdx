The Overview screen answers one question: **is the platform carrying what it should, and is it landing?**
It is deliberately not a health screen. Whether the software has the memory and connections to keep
working is a different question, asked on [Runtime](/platform/control-panel/runtime-metrics), and the
two are kept apart because an operator staring at a delivery problem does not want to be reading
about heap size.

Everything on the page obeys one range control — **24 hours**, **7 days** or **30 days**, defaulting
to 24 hours. The page asks for fresh figures every thirty seconds, and stops asking while the browser
tab is in the background.

## When the figures were read

From 0.12.16 the figures are a **snapshot the panel builds in the background**, not a query run for
your tab. A line under the title says when: **as of 10:42:17** means the panel last read the
database at that second, in your browser's time zone. Open the page in three tabs, or three
operators open it, and the database does the same work as for one.

The snapshot is rebuilt once a minute. Most of a minute's rebuild is small: the last three hours
and the current one are re-read, and so is today; everything older is kept from a full rebuild,
which happens every fifteen minutes. The failure breakdown is re-read every five minutes. So a
message submitted just now appears within a minute, and a receipt that changes the outcome of a
message from six hours ago is reflected within a quarter of an hour. On a gateway that keeps the
hourly usage summary (0.12.16, see below) the whole 24-hour view is re-read every minute, because
there it is cheap.

Two words can appear beside the time:

- **stale** — the last read of the named section failed (the database was unreachable, or a query
  timed out) and what you see is the reading before it. The time still advances, because it says when
  the panel last *tried*. A stale badge that persists is a database problem, not a traffic one.
- **Settling** — in the traffic chart's legend, with the most recent part of the chart shaded.
  Messages in the shaded hours or days may still receive a receipt, so their delivered and failed
  counts are not final. The shading covers the gateway's receipt deadline (`smsg.cdr.deadline.hours`,
  48 hours by default), so on the 24-hour view it is the whole chart, which is the truth: nothing
  on that view has settled yet.

The first request after the panel starts has nothing to serve and builds the snapshot while you
wait; if that takes longer than eight seconds the page says it is warming up and asks again on its
next poll.

The cadence is a panel setting, `OVERVIEW_REFRESH_SECONDS` in the panel's environment: 60 by default,
never below 15, and `0` turns the snapshot off and builds the figures on every request — what the
automated test rig uses, and not a setting for a busy platform.

### Where the figures come from

The 7- and 30-day views are built from the gateway's **daily usage summary** for every whole day it
has summarised, with only today read from the call records — see
[Usage and retention](/platform/billing/usage-and-retention#the-dashboards-read-it). From 0.12.16 the
gateway also keeps an **hourly usage summary**, brought up to date every minute as traffic flows, and
a panel on such a gateway reads the 24-hour view and today's part of the longer views from it rather
than from the records: 24 small rows plus the last minute or two of traffic, whatever the volume. Both
summaries are the gateway's work, switched on by default and tuned on the gateway settings page under
**Usage summaries**; the daily one is now written by the gateway itself each night rather than by a
command somebody has to run.

Three consequences worth knowing. On a summarised day **In flight** reads zero, because the daily
summary does not record what was still pending; today's figure is the live one. A message the gateway
retried across a minute boundary can be counted twice in the hourly summary until the gateway's
quarter-hourly recount of recent hours corrects it — retries are a small fraction of traffic and the
recount is the truth. And a gateway with neither summary reads everything live and slows as the range
grows, as before.

## The four figures

| Card | What it counts |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Messages** | Everything submitted in the period |
| **Delivery rate** | Delivered against failed, with both counts beneath |
| **In flight** | Submitted, no receipt yet |
| **Margin** | Billed less vendor cost, in red when negative |

<Note>
**A blank card is still loading, not zero.** The cards show a placeholder until the figure arrives,
and then show either the number or the error. They will never invent a `0` to fill the gap, because
"no traffic" and "we could not ask" are different answers and only one of them is your problem.
</Note>

**In flight is not a backlog.** It is traffic that has been sent and billed and simply has not been
reported on yet. A message that never gets a receipt is closed at its expiry deadline; until then it
sits here, and on a busy platform it always will.

## Traffic

Three series — submitted, delivered, failed — over the chosen range. With nothing in the window the
chart is replaced by **No traffic in this period**, which is a statement about the range you picked
as much as about the platform. Hours on the 24-hour view are clock hours in UTC; days on the longer
views are billing days, cut in the platform's billing zone. The shaded part of the chart is still
settling (see above).

## Why messages failed

A breakdown of every outcome that was not a delivery, each named in plain language rather than by its
receipt code:

- Failed at the vendor
- Rejected on submission
- Dropped before sending
- No receipt within the deadline

Beside each name, in smaller type, sits the numeric receipt state and the vendor's own error code.
That looks like duplication and is not. `EXPIRED`, `REJECTD` and `UNDELIV` all bill identically and
mean entirely different things, and when you go to a vendor to ask about a spike, **the number is
what their documentation is indexed by**. The name is for you; the number is for them.

### Dismissing a failure

Each row can be dismissed, which hides it from the list without deleting anything. A footer counts
what you have hidden and can show it again.

<Tip>
**A dismissed failure comes back on its own the next time it happens.** Dismissing hides what you
have already dealt with, not what starts again.
</Tip>

Dismissals live in the browser you made them in. Another operator, or the same operator on another
machine, sees the full list — which is deliberate, since one person's triage is not a platform-wide
statement of fact.

That distinction shows up in the two empty states. **No failures in this period** means nothing
failed. **Every failure in this period has been dismissed** means something did, and you chose not to
look at it. The screen will not report a clean period on the strength of your own filing.

## Gateway health

A strip of six tiles at the foot of the page — memory, processor, tasks running, API requests,
internal tasks, uptime — each with a sparkline, and a link through to the detail. It is a glance, not
an investigation.

<Warning>
**"Internal tasks" is not vendor connections.** It is the gateway's own background work: database
writes, receipt handling. Everywhere else in the panel a *worker* means an SMPP connection, so this
tile avoids the word entirely. Reading it as a stalled vendor sends you looking for a carrier problem
that does not exist.
</Warning>

The **API requests** tile reports a mean since the process started. It answers "is this endpoint slow
in general" and cannot answer "was it slow five minutes ago".

If a refresh fails while older figures are still on screen, a footnote says the poll failed and the
numbers stay put. The screen prefers a stale reading you are told about to a blank one you are not.
