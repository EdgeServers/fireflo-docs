title: "Refusals"

---

A **refusal** is a message FireFlo declined before routing. It is recorded in `cdr_rejected`, and it
is a different animal from a message that routed and failed.

```bash
psql "$FIREFLO_DB_URL" -c "SELECT reason, count(*), max(rejected_at) FROM cdr_rejected GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 2 DESC"
```

Or, without SQL, the **Refused** list in the control panel — which carries the same rows with the
request that produced them.

## The reasons

| `reason` | What happened | Customer saw | Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `headerNotApproved` | The sender ID is not on the account's approved list | `0x415` / `403` | Approve the sender ID |
| `templateNotMatched` | The body matched no approved template | `0x412` / `403` | Approve the wording, or fix the template |
| `missingMandatoryTlv` | A required tag resolved to nothing | `0xC3` / `403` | Supply it somewhere — see [TLVs](/platform/connections/tlvs) |
| `insufficientCredit` | Prepaid balance would not cover it | `0x402` / `402` | Top up |
| `noUnits` | A login of a [units account](/platform/billing/units-accounts) has no units of its product left | `0x403` / `402` `NO_UNITS` | Give it units, from the platform that manages it |
| `unitsPending` | Its units were held by another node for a moment | `0x14` / `503` `UNITS_PENDING` | Nothing: a retry goes through |
| `creditPending` | From 0.17.x. A funded account's credit was not confirmed within the wait limit | `0x58` (`conf.status.creditPending`) / `503` `CREDIT_PENDING` | Nothing for the customer: a resend goes through. Look at the database — see [below](#credit-not-confirmed-in-time) |
| `creditQueueFull` | From 0.17.x. Too many of the account's messages were already waiting for their credit | `0x14` / `503`, as a full queue | The same cause as `creditPending`, at volume |
| `queueFull` | The router queue was at its limit | `0x14` / `503` | Find why the queue is not draining — usually a vendor |
| `filtered` | A filter refused it | varies | Read the filter |
| `maxAttempts` | **Not a refusal.** Routing gave up | nothing at submit time | [Nothing is delivered](/platform/troubleshooting/nothing-is-delivered) |

The SMPP column is what the customer's `submit_sm_resp` carried; the HTTP column is the REST
equivalent. Most are **vendor-specific** codes in the `0x4xx` range rather than SMPP
standard ones — the exceptions are `0xC3`, which is `ESME_RMISSINGOPTPARAM` from the specification,
and `0x14` and `0x58`. A
customer's SMPP client will not have names for the `0x4xx` values, so quote the reason word to them,
not the number.

## What every refusal has in common

**Nothing was charged, or the charge was reversed.** A refusal costs the customer nothing, which is
why they are safe to be strict about — and why `insufficientCredit` is not a paradox.

## The one that is hardest to explain

<Warning>
**A multipart content refusal happens *after* every segment was acknowledged.**

On SMPP, content is checked against a template only once the segments are reassembled — at submit
time the body is still a fragment, and `"Your OTP is {#var#}, valid"` matches no part of a two-part
message.

So the customer's client saw an OK for every segment, and the message was then dropped, refunded and
recorded. There is no error they could have caught, and the receipt is the only signal.

This is the normal shape for Indian-language DLT templates, where UCS-2 puts 67 characters in a
segment — so a template that works in English starts failing the moment the wording is translated.
</Warning>

## Reading a `templateNotMatched`

Almost always one of four things, in descending order of how often it is actually the cause:

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="The wording genuinely differs">
Whitespace is folded before matching, so runs of spaces and newlines are not the problem. Anything
else — an extra full stop, a changed word, a different variable position — is.

The panel's template preview shows a sample message the template accepts. Compare it against what the
customer actually sent, which is on the `cdr_rejected` row.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The template is degraded">
An unrecognised placeholder name — `{#VAR#}`, `{#otp#}` — loads as a **generic variable** rather than
being dropped, and the row is reported under `templates_degraded` on `/ops/health`.

It still matches, but far more loosely than intended: `{#var#}` accepts anything at all up to the
length limit. If a template is approving messages it should not, look here first.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The variable is longer than the limit">
`smsg.whitelist.max.variable` bounds how much text one placeholder may stand for. A template of
`"Your code is {#var#}"` with a 900-character body is refused even though the wording matches.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The account is in ENFORCE and should not be">
`REPORT` mode counts what *would* be refused without refusing it — `whitelist_would_reject` on
`/ops/health`. Running a new account in `REPORT` for a day before enforcing turns this whole class of
incident into a number you read at your leisure.
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>

## Credit not confirmed in time

`creditPending` is the refusal a customer with money on the account asks about. It is transient, it
costs them nothing, and the fix is almost never on their side.

**How credit reaches a message.** The gateway does not go to the database for every message. It
reserves an account's credit in **blocks** — sized from what the account sent in the last ten
seconds, never less than `smsg.balance.block.min.messages` messages' worth (20 by default) — and
spends from the block in memory. A message that finds no credit in hand **waits** for the next block
instead of being refused, and is charged as soon as it lands. If a reservation fails because the
database is slow or does not answer, the gateway tries again while the message waits.

Only when the wait runs out is the message refused, as `creditPending`:

| How it was sent | What the sender receives |
| :--- | :--- |
| SMPP | `submit_sm_resp` with `0x58` (`ESME_RTHROTTLED`) — the listener's `conf.status.creditPending`. Before 0.17.x it was `0x14`. |
| REST | `503`, code `CREDIT_PENDING`, `Retry-After: 2`. Before 0.17.x a `503` with no code, recorded as `queueFull`. |
| gRPC | `UNAVAILABLE`, `ErrorDetail.code` `CREDIT_PENDING`, retry after 2 seconds. Before 0.17.x `QUEUE_FULL`, recorded as `queueFull`. |
| Platform API | Send status `CREDIT_PENDING`. |

**Nothing is charged**, and **resending works**: both `0x58` and a `503` tell a client to wait and send
again, and the resend is charged once, normally.

**The wait** is `smsg.balance.park.seconds`, gateway-wide: **10 seconds** by default, 1 to 30. A send
over the HTTP API, gRPC or the platform API waits at most 5 seconds of it, because the caller's own
timeout is usually not much longer. See [Limits](/reference/config/limits#waiting-for-credit).

**The causes**, in the order they occur:

1. **The gateway's database is slow, or is not answering.** A block cannot be reserved until it does.
   Every account on the gateway that runs out of block during the stall waits together, so these
   refusals come in a cluster across many accounts. Before 0.17.x a reservation that failed refused
   the waiting messages at once, and the wait was at most 2 seconds.
2. **On gateways before 0.15.9, several messages arriving together after a quiet period.** Those
   versions refused a message that found no block in hand at once, so a customer sending in bursts —
   a batch every half minute, say — was refused part of every burst while the next block was
   reserved. 0.15.9 made such a message wait (at most 2 seconds), and 0.17.x made the wait settable
   and the reservation retried.

`creditQueueFull` is the same cause at volume: when **10,000** of one account's messages, or
**100,000** across the gateway, are already waiting for credit, the next is refused at once — `0x14`
over SMPP, a full-queue `503` over the API — rather than joining a queue that is not moving. Nothing is
charged and a resend works once the database answers.

**What to look at:**

- The **Refused** page in the control panel: the count under **Credit not confirmed in time; resend**
  (and **Too many waiting for credit; resend**) for the period, and whether it is one account or
  many. Many accounts in the same minutes point at the database.
- The database's response time over the same period. A database answering in a few milliseconds does
  not produce these.
- The gateway log. `balance.credit.parked` is written when an account's messages had to wait for their
  credit, with how long they waited and how many there were; `balance.credit.pending` when credit could
  not be reserved in time and messages were refused `creditPending`.

<Warning>
**Keep `smsg.balance.park.seconds` below your customers' SMPP response timeout.** A client that stops
waiting before the gateway answers resends, and if the first submit is then accepted, both copies are
sent. 10 seconds is below the usual client defaults; raise it only knowing what your customers' clients
wait.
</Warning>

## `insufficientCredit` on an account with money on it

`insufficientCredit` means the reservation found no money to cover the message. A message waiting for
its block is never refused this way.

**The floor never strands money.** A reservation tries **three descending rungs**: the full block, the
`smsg.balance.block.min.messages` floor, then the single message that asked. An account funded for
three messages sends three.

<Tip>
**Persistent `insufficientCredit` with a healthy balance** is usually credit held in `reserved` — by
another node, or stranded by a `kill -9`. Check the ledger and `reserved`; see
[Prepaid credit](/platform/billing/prepaid-credit).
</Tip>
